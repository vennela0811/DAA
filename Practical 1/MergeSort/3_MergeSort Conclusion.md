Merge Sort is a classic divide-and-conquer algorithm that recursively divides the input array into halves and merges them back in sorted order.
It guarantees a strictly optimal time complexity of $O(n \log n)$ across best, average, and worst-case scenarios.
It requires $O(n)$ auxiliary space to handle the temporary arrays during the merge step and remains a stable sorting method.
While memory-intensive, its predictable efficiency makes it exceptionally reliable for large data structures and external sorting.
In DAA, it serves as the premier illustration of the divide-and-conquer paradigm, showcasing logarithmic depth recurrence relations.
