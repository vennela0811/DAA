Binary Search is an efficient divide-and-conquer search algorithm that repeatedly halves the search space of a sorted dataset.
It guarantees a logarithmic time complexity of $O(\log n)$ for average and worst cases, along with an $O(1)$ best-case runtime.
It requires minimal auxiliary memory, achieving an in-place space complexity of $O(1)$ iteratively or $O(\log n)$ recursively.
Its core operational constraint is that the underlying collection must be strictly sorted beforehand.
In DAA, it serves as the quintessential example of logarithmic search efficiency, contrasting sharply with sequential linear scans.
